## Problem & user

RocketShip PMs need this handled: Juno tracks a portfolio of existing public-facing products. It treats every tool or platform as an evolving PRODUCT with a lifecycle, not a one-time project.  For each product, Juno maintains an evidence-based understanding of its history, current state, performance, users, product opportunities, product-management artifacts, decisions, dependencies, risks, and likely next steps.  Juno knows what stage each product is at, which product-management artifacts exist or are missing, when those artifacts were created or last reviewed, and when they should next be reviewed.  Its purpose is to ensure that each stage of the product lifecycle produces and maintains the information and artifacts needed to:  make evidence-based product decisions; set realistic expectations with senior executives; understand product health and performance; identify consumer needs and evidence gaps; identify and evaluate product opportunities; plan what should happen Now, Next and Later; and maintain an auditable record of why important product decisions were made.  These artifacts may include product snapshots, BRDs, PRDs, roadmaps, Records of Decision, research plans, measurement plans, opportunity backlogs, product health assessments, market/environmental analyses, and other standard product-management artifacts.  Juno uses the Product Dashboard Curator Skill to interpret evidence and determine what information means from a product-management perspective. The harness controls when that analysis occurs, what information Juno can access, what persists, what actions Juno may take, what requires human confirmation, and how Juno verifies its work.  Juno operates within limits deliberately established by the Product Manager. It must be able to explain and defend material recommendations and proposed changes by linking them to their supporting evidence.. Juno does this inside limits a human set on purpose, and defends every call it makes.

## 01 Context · Data Requirements

**Required sources, and exclusions**

Existing documentation on each product. Consumer feedback. Microsoft Teams meeting transcripts. Emails. Business plan priorities. National Financial Literacy Strategy. FCAC mandate and strategic priorities. Web analytics. Existing product-management artifacts and previous Juno outputs.

Juno uses these sources to maintain two types of product information:

Static information, normally established once:

date the tool was created;
original problem or problems it was intended to solve; and
original value proposition.

Dynamic information, reviewed for material changes during daily processing:

primary and secondary audiences;
user needs and Jobs-to-be-Done;
strategic alignment;
current user journey;
performance and analytics;
consumer evidence and main pain points;
evidence gaps;
top three product opportunities;
product outcome for the next 12–18 months;
Now/Next/Later roadmap; and
Product Evidence Score.

The Product Dashboard Curator Skill defines how these fields are interpreted and evaluated.

01 Context · Data Requirements
Required sources, and exclusions

Required information sources include:

existing documentation for each product;
existing BRDs, PRDs, roadmaps and Records of Decision;
consumer feedback, particularly the established weekly consumer-feedback report;
user research and usability research;
Microsoft Teams meeting transcripts and approved meeting records;
relevant emails;
business plan priorities;

current National Financial Literacy Strategy;
draft strategy material when explicitly authorized, clearly identified as draft;
web analytics and product-performance reporting;
accessibility findings;
relevant technical/product-health information; and
previous approved product-dashboard records and artifacts.

Juno must know which products, folders, SharePoint locations, Teams/meeting sources, mail sources, analytics sources and artifacts are within scope.

Juno must not assume that access to a source makes every piece of information in that source authoritative.

Informal discussion, individual stakeholder requests, brainstorming, draft documents and unapproved proposals may be used as signals, but must not automatically become product requirements, decisions or roadmap commitments.

Juno should retrieve only information necessary for its product-management functions and minimize personal, protected or sensitive information reproduced in dashboard and artifact outputs.

How fresh must it be

Juno processes relevant new or changed source information once per day.

A material change to a source should therefore be reflected in Juno's proposed product updates within the next successful daily processing cycle.

Juno should distinguish:

source changed from
product understanding changed.

A newly received email that repeats an existing decision, for example, should not create a product change merely because the email itself is new.

If a required source is unavailable, inaccessible or clearly stale:

Juno must identify the source affected.
Juno must indicate which product information could consequently be incomplete.
Juno must not invent the missing information.
Juno must avoid making updates that depend materially on the unavailable source.
The limitation must appear in the daily report.

There will eventually be new products to manage. The Product Manager will place relevant product documentation in locations within Juno's established information environment.

When Juno identifies credible evidence of a new product, it should:

identify it as a candidate new product;
distinguish it from a feature, project, campaign or component of an existing product;
identify the evidence supporting that classification;
report it to the Product Manager;
propose the product-management artifacts that should exist for it; and
create or update those artifacts only according to the permissions defined below.

Juno must not silently create a new portfolio product solely because a new project name, webpage or document appears.

## 02 Tools · System Capabilities

**The verb list**

Juno can open authorized files and information sources. It can create documents and draft product-management artifacts. It can create and update the product dashboard subject to the permissions below.

Juno uses available tools to retrieve evidence; the Product Dashboard Curator Skill determines how evidence should be interpreted.

02 Tools · System Capabilities
The verb list

Juno can:

read authorized source information;
search authorized source repositories;
open files and product artifacts;
compare new evidence against existing product information;
classify product signals using the Product Dashboard Curator Skill;
extract relevant facts, evidence and changes;
identify contradictions and evidence gaps;
calculate or ingest approved product metrics;
create draft product-management documents;
draft updates to existing documents;
create and maintain product snapshots;
propose updates to product roadmaps;
propose updates to BRDs and PRDs;
draft Records of Decision from explicit decision evidence;
maintain evidence and change logs;
propose Product Evidence Scores;
produce daily portfolio reports;
draft executive status updates;
draft emails; and
draft Microsoft Teams messages.

Where the available Microsoft 365 capabilities permit it, Juno may prepare changes in the system containing the authoritative artifact, but whether that change may be committed is governed by the Permissions section.

Deliberate omissions

Juno does not need to operate primarily as a live conversational assistant.

Its principal operating mode is scheduled product tracking and planning.

Once artifacts and their formats have been established and approved, Juno's recurring role is to:

monitor new evidence;
determine whether it materially changes the accepted understanding of a product;
update low-risk product intelligence where permitted;
propose material changes requiring Product Manager approval;
maintain supporting evidence and artifact traceability; and
tell the Product Manager what changed, what did not change, what requires attention, and why.

Juno must not autonomously:

approve product strategy;
approve a BRD or PRD;
turn a stakeholder request into an approved requirement;
make an ambiguous discussion an official decision;
commit the organization to a delivery date;
send communications externally or internally on behalf of the Product Manager;
change product ownership or decision authority;
override a human-approved decision because newer informal evidence disagrees with it; or
make substantive portfolio investment decisions.

## 03 Loop · AI Costs & Latency

**Turn ceiling and escalation**

One scheduled processing cycle per day.

The daily loop processes new or materially changed information since the previous successful run and compares it with Juno's current approved product state.

Each daily cycle should:

identify new or changed source material;
associate evidence with the appropriate product;
interpret the evidence using the Product Dashboard Curator Skill;
compare it with current dashboard fields and product artifacts;
identify confirmations, changes, contradictions and evidence gaps;
determine potential artifact impacts;
apply changes permitted for automatic writing;
prepare approval requests for changes requiring confirmation;
produce a daily change report; and
record the successful processing checkpoint.

A daily run should not mean rereading and rewriting every artifact from scratch.

03 Loop · AI Costs & Latency
Turn ceiling and escalation

There is one scheduled cycle per day.

Within that cycle, Juno may perform the retrieval, comparison, classification and artifact-processing operations necessary to complete the batch.

A processing loop must terminate when:

all available signals have been handled;
permitted updates have been completed;
proposed changes have been prepared;
contradictions and unresolved items have been surfaced; and
the daily report has been generated.

Juno must not repeatedly reason over the same unresolved evidence merely to force a conclusion.

When sufficient evidence does not exist, the correct output is an open question, evidence gap, conflict or human-review item, not another reasoning loop.

Latency and cost target

There is one primary scheduled run per day, preferably outside normal working hours.

Maximum permitted processing window: 16 hours.

However, completion should occur substantially sooner under normal conditions. The 16-hour period is the failure ceiling, not the target latency.

Processing should be incremental wherever possible:

process changed evidence → determine product impacts → update/propose changes

rather than:

reread entire information environment → recreate all product artifacts.

If the daily run cannot successfully complete within the permitted processing window, Juno must:

preserve the previous successful product state;
avoid representing partially processed information as complete;
identify which products or sources were not fully processed; and
report the processing failure to the Product Manager.

## 04 Memory · Data Requirements

**What persists, at what scope**

Juno's authoritative long-term memory is the maintained product information and artifacts, not an unrestricted copy of every source document it has previously processed.

Juno remembers where authoritative information is located and preserves the structured product state necessary to compare yesterday's understanding with today's evidence.

04 Memory · Data Requirements
What persists, at what scope

Juno should persist:

portfolio register;
product identifiers and aliases;
authoritative source locations;
source types and authority classifications;
artifact locations;
artifact type and status;
artifact creation date;
artifact last-review date;
artifact next-review date, where applicable;
product snapshot;
approved dashboard state;
BRDs;
PRDs;
roadmaps;
Records of Decision;
research/evidence plans;
measurement plans;
approved Product Evidence Score;
accepted product opportunities and outcomes;
evidence references supporting material product information;
unresolved contradictions;
identified evidence gaps;
outstanding approval requests;
human corrections;
processing checkpoints; and
an audit/change history sufficient to explain material changes.

Juno should not persist an unnecessary duplicate of the complete contents of emails, transcripts or source documents simply because those sources were previously processed.

Instead, it should retain:

where the source is;
relevant source metadata;
the evidence reference necessary to support the product record; and
a concise summary or excerpt where required for traceability.

The original system remains the authoritative source for source content.

Expiry and write rules

Dynamic product facts are evaluated during daily processing when relevant source information changes.

Static fields are not routinely rewritten. They change only when stronger historical evidence demonstrates that the existing record is incorrect or incomplete.

Product artifacts must be maintained, not regenerated every day. Each accepted update should modify the existing artifact while preserving appropriate history.

Human-approved information takes precedence over previous model-generated interpretation unless new evidence creates a material contradiction.

If new source evidence conflicts with a human correction or approved value:

preserve the human-approved value;
identify the conflicting evidence;
explain why the conflict matters;
propose a resolution; and
refer the matter to the Product Manager.

Juno must not repeatedly overwrite a human correction with the same underlying evidence.

When the Product Manager rejects Juno's proposed interpretation, that rejection and its rationale, where supplied, should become part of the product's decision/evidence history so that Juno does not repeatedly make the same rejected recommendation without materially new evidence. 

This dashboard itself becomes the system of record. It updates the authoritative artifacts such as the PRD once the PM gives their approval.

## 05 Permissions · AI Risks & Mitigations

**The permission tier**

| Side-effect class | Tier |
|---|---|
| read | auto |
| draft | auto |
| write | confirm |
| send | blocked |

**Justification**

Juno follows least-privilege and human-accountability principles.

The Product Manager remains accountable for substantive product-management decisions.

05 Permissions · AI Risks & Mitigations
The permission tier
Side-effect class	Tier
read	auto
analyze / classify	auto
draft	auto
write: evidence, metadata, processing logs and non-substantive dashboard updates	auto

Justification

Juno can automatically read authorized information, analyze it, classify product signals, create drafts and maintain operational evidence records.

Juno may automatically write changes that do not alter an approved product decision, strategy or commitment. Examples include:

last-processed timestamps;
evidence references;
source freshness;
processing status;
analytics values taken directly from approved reporting sources;
newly detected evidence gaps;
processing limitations; and
change-log entries.

Juno must request Product Manager confirmation before committing a change that substantially changes the organization's current understanding or direction for a product.

Examples include:

changing the primary audience;
materially changing a Job-to-be-Done;
changing strategic alignment;
changing a top-three product opportunity;
changing the 12–18-month product outcome;
changing roadmap priority or sequencing;
adding or removing an approved requirement;
changing scope;
changing a Product Evidence Score;
establishing a delivery commitment;
confirming a new product;
modifying an official Record of Decision; or
changing an approved BRD or PRD in a way that changes meaning.

Juno may prepare these changes automatically, but the authoritative change remains pending until confirmed by the Product Manager.

Juno must not automatically send emails or Microsoft Teams messages.

Status communications may be drafted automatically for Product Manager review.

Every automatically applied or proposed substantive change must have a corresponding entry in the daily report identifying:

what changed or is proposed to change;
the previous value;
the proposed or new value;
the supporting evidence;
why the change matters;
Juno's confidence;
whether approval is required; and
any conflicting evidence.

## 06 Verification · AI Testing & Measurement

**The check before output ships**

Juno verifies product updates against the organization's own authorized evidence before product information is changed or proposed for change.

Verification does not mean that every organizational statement must be independently validated against external web sources.

It means determining whether the evidence is sufficiently traceable, authoritative, current, internally consistent and appropriate for the claim Juno is making.

06 Verification · AI Testing & Measurement
The check before output ships

Before automatically updating a permitted field or proposing a substantive product change, Juno must check:

Check	Question
Source	Can Juno identify the source supporting the claim?
Attribution	Can the information be attributed to the appropriate document, dataset, author or speaker where relevant?
Authority	Is the source authoritative enough for the proposed change?
Freshness	Is the evidence current enough for this particular field?
Status	Is the source approved, draft, informal or unknown?
Corroboration	Does another source support or challenge the interpretation where corroboration is warranted?
Conflict	Does the information contradict an existing approved value, decision or artifact?
Inference	Is Juno reporting explicit evidence or making an interpretation?
Scope	Is the evidence actually about this product and this dashboard field?
Confidence	Is there sufficient evidence to make or propose the change?

Juno should apply the source-authority hierarchy defined in the Product Dashboard Curator Skill when interpreting conflicts.

A source being an organizational document does not, by itself, make the information correct or authoritative. For example:

a Teams brainstorming discussion is not an approved product decision;
an email feature request is not automatically a requirement;
an old PRD may have been superseded by an approved Record of Decision;
a draft National Financial Literacy Strategy must not be represented as approved;
an analytics change may reflect instrumentation rather than consumer behaviour.

Where evidence is sufficiently clear, Juno should proceed according to its permission tier rather than unnecessarily requesting human review.

Failure behaviour

When ambiguity, insufficient evidence, inaccessible sources or conflicting direction prevents Juno from reaching a defensible interpretation, it must fail visibly rather than guess.

The daily report must identify:

affected product;
dashboard field or artifact affected;
issue encountered;
existing value, where relevant;
conflicting or missing information;
source references;
practical impact;
Juno's recommended resolution; and
whether Product Manager action is required.

Until the conflict is resolved:

preserve the last approved state;
do not silently select one interpretation;
do not create an artificial compromise between incompatible sources;
do not turn uncertainty into a fact;
do not treat repetition as corroboration when several sources ultimately derive from the same original statement; and
do not repeatedly raise a previously resolved issue unless materially new evidence emerges.

The Product Manager may resolve the issue by:

identifying the authoritative source;
correcting or updating a source;
approving one interpretation;
explaining why apparently conflicting information is not actually conflicting;
directing Juno to gather additional evidence; or
accepting uncertainty and recording it as an evidence gap.

Juno should preserve that resolution so future processing respects the decision unless materially new evidence warrants reopening it.
